    Merge Shapes turns two or more overlapping shapes into new shapes, like PowerPoint's feature of the same name.
    
      
      
        
    1. Draw two or more shapes so that they overlap.
      2. 
        
    2. Pick them: click the first one, then Shift+click the others (or drag a box around them). On a phone or tablet, turn on Pick several (the two-squares button on the tool bar) and tap each one.
      3. 
        
    3. Choose one of the five buttons under Merge Shapes in the Inspector.
      4. 
      
    
      
    The order you pick them in matters: the first shape you pick gives the result its colours, and Subtract keeps the first shape and cuts the others out of it. (Fragment is the exception: each piece keeps the colour it already had.) Little numbers on the canvas and in the Layers list show the order.
    
      
      
      
      Shape builder (K) does the same jobs by hand: pick the overlapping shapes, choose the Shape builder, then drag across the pieces you want joined into one shape. Hold Alt (Option on a Mac) and drag to delete pieces instead, or click one piece to make it a shape of its own. Escape goes back to Select.
      
      
      Merging works on filled shapes: rectangles, ellipses, polygons, stars, shields, pen shapes and earlier merges - and on text. Text is turned into shapes first (its letter outlines, holes and all), so a word can be cut out of a shield or joined to a star; Undo brings the text back. That works with the included fonts, not the ones built into the computer (Sans, Serif, Mono, Rounded, Impact), which a web page cannot read. Lines and pencil squiggles join in too: their outline becomes a filled band first (Outline to shape, in the Outline section, does that on its own). Groups and pictures are left out (ungroup a group first). To show a picture inside a shape or a word, use Clip to top shape instead (under Align and arrange).
      
    
      
    
      
      
      VCTREDTR  is a vector drawing app. Draw shapes, paths and text, then merge shapes the way PowerPoint does: Union, Combine, Fragment, Intersect and Subtract.
      
      
      Private by design. Your drawings never leave this device. There are no uploads, accounts or analytics, and the page's security policy blocks it from sending data anywhere.
      
      
      The only outside code is the drawing engine, Paper.js 0.12.18 by Jürg Lehni and Jonathan Puckey (MIT licence). It is downloaded from jsDelivr, checked against a fixed fingerprint, then kept for offline use.
      
      
      The fonts for text ship with the app, in its fonts folder: free, open-licence fonts from the Google Fonts collection (SIL Open Font License 1.1; Luckiest Guy and Permanent Marker, Apache License 2.0), by their makers named in fonts/LICENSES.txt. They load from this app's own address, never from a font service.
      
      
      Projects. Save project keeps everything editable in a .vctredtr file. Export makes a PNG picture or an SVG file for other apps.
