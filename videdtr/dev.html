    Export

    A .vidproj file is your edit, not your video. It records every clip, cut, crop and fade as plain JSON - usually a few kilobytes. Small enough to email, to keep in version control, or to read in a text editor.
    It does not contain your media. Opening one asks you to point at the original files again, matched by name and size. That is why it stays tiny.
    Final Cut Pro 7 XML is the format both Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve have read for years. Your tracks, clips, cuts and source in and out points come across frame-accurately.
    A browser is never told where your files actually are, so we cannot fill this in for you. Give the folder and the XML will point straight at your media; leave it blank and the clips import offline for you to relink by name, which both applications handle well.
    What transfers, and what does not. Timing, track layout, clip names and source ranges are exact. Scale, rotation, position, crop, opacity and audio levels are written as standard motion filters and should land close, but I have not been able to test them against a real copy of Premiere or Resolve - check them on arrival. Text overlays have no equivalent and are skipped; the file lists them in a comment so you know what to rebuild.
    An EDL is the oldest and most portable thing here. It is a plain-text list of cuts that essentially every editing system on earth can read, including Premiere, Resolve, Avid and Final Cut.
    The format only describes one track of straight cuts. No effects, no crops, no fades, no layering - just which piece of which file plays when. That limitation is why it is so universally readable.

      VIDEDTR

      Everything happens on this device. Your video is never uploaded. The page is not even permitted to make a network connection — that is enforced by the browser, not just promised by the code.

      Getting started

      1. Drop video, audio or image files into the Media Pool.
      2. Click a tile to see its name and details, download it, or add it at the playhead. Drag one onto a track instead and a dashed outline shows exactly where it will land before you let go.
      3. Drag a clip's ends to trim it; drag its middle to move it.
      4. Select a clip and drag it straight on the preview: corners resize, edges crop, and the handle on the stem rotates.
      5. Select a clip and use the Inspector to size, crop, fade or adjust volume.
      6. Everything you can add lives behind the Add button above the tracks - new tracks in the top group, things that drop at the playhead in the bottom one.
      7. Reorder tracks by dragging a track header, or with its up and down arrows. The track at the top is drawn in front of the ones below it, so that is how you put a caption or a logo over your footage.
      8. Press Export when you are happy.

      Installing it

      Served over https, this installs as an app: look for the install icon in Chrome or Edge's address bar, or use Share → Add to Home Screen on iOS. It then opens in its own window with no browser furniture. Dropping the optional sw.js beside this file adds the automatic install prompt and lets it open with no network at all.

      If the tab closes

      Your edit is copied into this browser's local storage a few seconds after every change, and again when you switch away from the tab. Reopen the page and it offers to pick up where you left off. The media itself is not stored - there is nowhere near enough room - so restoring asks you to point at your files again. Project settings has a button to wipe the backup, which is worth knowing about on a shared machine.

      On the timeline

      Clip bodyDrag to move it, along the track or onto another one.
      Clip endsDrag to trim. The clip gets shorter; nothing is thrown away.
      Top cornersThe small squares set a fade. The white ramp drawn across the clip shows how long it is and which way it goes.
      Track nameDouble-click to rename it. Enter keeps the change, Esc abandons it.
      Amber diamondsWhen a clip animates, these are its two marks. Click one to choose which position you are setting - it turns white. Drag it to change when the move starts or finishes.
      Track orderDrag a header, or use its arrows. The top track draws in front.
      Duplicate a trackThe copy button in the header. The copy lands directly below the original with all its clips, crops, filters and fades intact.

      Handles on the preview

      The handles and the Inspector sliders change the same numbers. Use whichever suits the moment - handles to rough something in, sliders when you want an exact value or are working from the keyboard.

      Inside the boxDrag to move. Hold Shift to snap back to centre.
      Corner squareResize evenly. The clip keeps its proportions.
      MirrorHorizontal and vertical flips live in the Picture section. They are applied after any rotation, so flipping a tilted clip mirrors what you can see.
      Edge barCrop that edge. The opposite edge stays where it is.
      Stem handleRotate. Hold Shift for 15° steps; it snaps to square on its own near 0, 90 and 180°.

      The Inspector

      Sections collapse, and only one is open at a time - with this many controls a single long scroll meant the thing you wanted was never on screen. Whichever section you last opened stays open as you move between clips, so working through several of them adjusting colour does not mean reopening COLOUR every time. A blue dot on a closed section means something in there has been changed from its default, and the ? beside a heading explains what that section does.

      Sizing and cropping

      ContainFits the whole picture inside the frame. Bars appear if the shapes differ.
      CoverFills the frame completely. Whatever hangs over the edge is cut off.
      StretchSquashes the picture to the frame exactly. Distorts it, but occasionally useful.
      Crop to frame shapeTrims evenly from opposite edges until what is left matches the project shape. This is how you get a wide photo into a vertical video without bars.
      Trim slidersCut into each edge by hand. Nothing is written to your file - cropping just narrows the window we look through.

      Shapes and shadows

      RectangleThe whole picture, with a corner radius if you want one.
      Square / CircleTakes the shorter side of the picture for both dimensions, so a circle is a true circle and never an oval. Move the picture underneath to choose what sits inside it.
      Drop shadowFollows whatever shape you chose, corners and circles included. Softness and offsets are percentages of the frame, so they look the same at every export size.

      Speed

      The Speed section keeps the same piece of footage and changes how long it takes to play, so the clip gets shorter or longer on the timeline while showing exactly the frames it showed before.

      Speeding upLeaves a gap where the clip used to end. Nothing else moves - which is the point, because sliding this track would knock it out of sync with the others. A Close the gap button appears when there is one to close, and it only ever moves clips on that same track.
      Slowing downThe clip grows, and whatever follows it on that track is pushed along to make room. Nothing is trimmed or lost.
      SoundPitch moves with speed, the way a tape machine does. The preview and the exported file always agree.
      On the clipAnything not running at normal speed carries a small badge like 2× in its corner.
      RangeA quarter speed up to four times. Beyond that browsers decode unreliably and the sound falls apart.
      ExportingA .vidproj keeps speed exactly. Premiere and Resolve XML carries it as a Time Remap; an EDL carries it as an M2 record.

      Moving a clip while it plays

      Select a clip and turn on Motion in the Inspector. Pick Start pose, drag the picture where it should begin; pick End pose, drag it where it should finish. It travels between the two amber diamonds on the clip, which you can drag to change the timing. This is how you slide a webcam inset out of the way to reveal what is behind it, without cutting the clip in two and having it jump.

      Hiding something: the blur track

      Blur on the timeline toolbar adds a blur region on its own track. It hides whatever is on the tracks below it, so drag the track up or down to choose what it covers. Drag the handles on the preview to place it, and round the corners all the way to get a circle. The strength slider has a floor: the weakest setting is still a real blur, so it cannot be turned off by accident.

      Colour

      Video and image clips get a COLOUR section in the Inspector: eight one-click looks, then sliders for brightness, contrast, saturation, black and white, hue and blur. The graphics card applies these while drawing, so the preview and the export are the same thing rather than two guesses at it. A clip with colour adjusted carries a small wand badge on the timeline.

      Light and dark

      The sun/moon button switches between them, and your choice is remembered for next time. Until you press it the app follows whatever your operating system is set to, and keeps following it - so if your machine switches over in the evening, this does too. Pressing the button once means you have an opinion, and it is honoured from then on. Both themes are built to WCAG 2.2 AAA contrast (7:1) for body text. The video itself is unaffected: exports look identical either way.

      Zooming the preview

      The pill in the corner of the preview zooms from 25% to 300%. The two halves of that range do different jobs, which is worth knowing:

      Below 100%Shows the area around the frame, so you can find a clip you have pushed off the edge. A dashed outline marks where the exported picture actually ends.
      Above 100%Magnifies, for checking exactly where two things overlap or how close a caption sits to an edge. It stops at 300%, which is where a 1080p frame runs out of real detail.
      SliderDrag for a big change; the − and + buttons step by a quarter each press.
      WheelCtrl (or Cmd) and scroll zooms towards the pointer. On a trackpad, pinch does the same thing. Plain scrolling pans, once there is something to pan to.
      PanningDrag any empty part of the stage. Over a clip the drag belongs to the clip, so use the middle mouse button there - or pinch and drag on a touchscreen.
      FitBack to 100% and centred.

      None of this touches the video. Zoom and pan are a magnifying glass over the preview; the exported file is identical whatever you leave them set to.

      Changing the shape of the whole project

      Project → Verticalize 9:16 turns a landscape edit into a tall one: the canvas changes and every clip is centred and cropped to fill it. Centring is only a starting point - split each clip where the action moves and set Position X on the pieces to follow it, or use Motion to pan between two points. Square and Widescreen work the same way.

      Four ways out

      Video fileRenders the finished thing to .mp4, or .webm where H.264 is unavailable. The cut is baked in.
      Project fileA few kilobytes of JSON holding your edit. Reopen it here later; it asks you to point at the media again.
      Premiere / ResolveFinal Cut Pro 7 XML. Tracks, cuts and source ranges transfer frame-accurately; effects are best-effort. Both applications have read this format for years.
      EDLOne track of straight cuts as plain text. No effects, but readable by essentially every editing system ever made.

      Known limits

      • Export needs the WebCodecs API. Chrome and Edge are the safe bets; Safari support is newer and patchier; Firefox varies by version.
      • Not every browser build includes H.264 and AAC, which are patented. Where they are missing, the export falls back to a .webm file using VP9 and Opus. It plays anywhere modern and converts to MP4 easily.
      • Decoded audio is held in memory so it can be mixed for export. Very long sources will use a lot of RAM.
      • Frame-accurate seeking depends on the source file. Variable frame rate recordings (phones, some screen recorders) can drift slightly.
      • Icons are Lucide (lucide.dev), used under the ISC licence and embedded directly rather than fetched.

      Reloading and updating

      Installed as an app, this has no address bar and no pull-to-refresh - there is no browser chrome for them to sit in - and it deliberately loads from its offline copy first so it opens with no network. Those two together mean an installed copy cannot notice a new version on its own. These buttons are how you make it look.
